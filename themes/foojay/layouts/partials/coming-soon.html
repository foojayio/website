{{/* "Coming Soon" -- the posts that are written and scheduled but not yet published. Reproduces the section the WordPress home page carries. WHY THIS READS THE FILESYSTEM INSTEAD OF RANGING OVER PAGES. `buildFuture` is false (Hugo's default, and unset in hugo.toml), so a post with a future `date:` is skipped ENTIRELY: no page, and no entry in site.Pages, any list, any term page, the feeds, sitemap.xml or the Pagefind index. That is exactly the semantics scheduling wants -- the article genuinely does not exist on the site until its day -- but it also means no template can `range` for these. So they are read as DATA, off disk, outside Hugo's page graph. THE ALTERNATIVE WAS REJECTED, and this is the reason to keep. Setting `buildFuture = true` would put the pages back in reach, at the price of a `where .Date "lt" now` in every list, term, feed and JSON-LD template plus the Pagefind body -- and ONE missed spot publishes an embargoed article early, silently. A dozen filters that must all agree is the tax this repo opens by refusing. Nothing here is authored. Scheduling a post is giving it a future `date:` and filing it under content/posts///
/ like every other post -- there is no `scheduled:` flag to set, and none to remember to unset once the post is out, which is how a "coming soon" article stays coming soon for ever. COMPARISON IS BY DAY, NOT BY INSTANT, because the publish TIME is not the author's to choose: every post goes out at the one daily build (.github/workflows/publish-scheduled.yml, 07:00 UTC), so a scheduled post is dated `YYYY-MM-DD` with no time at all and Frontmatter.checkPostDates rejects one that carries a time. Day granularity is also exactly Hugo's own rule here: a date-only post is midnight UTC, so "date after today" holds precisely while Hugo is still withholding it. The two cannot drift apart into a post that is neither listed here nor on the site.
