Foojay wouldn't exist without the many contributions from its community, whether it's a single post or an ongoing stream of knowledge sharing. That's why we highlight two of them each month as **[Featured Authors](/today/author/)**. After [Bazlur](/today/author/bazlur-rahman/) and [Nicolas](/today/author/nicolas-frankel/) in October, November belongs to **[Geertjan Wielenga](/today/author/geertjan-wielenga/)** and **[Ricardo Mello](/today/author/ricardo-mello/)**. Want to read more from either of them? Click their name to visit their Foojay author page, where you'll find their full list of posts.

## Geertjan Wielenga

**[Geertjan Wielenga](/today/author/geertjan-wielenga/)** is a Java Champion, an Apache Member, and the leader of the Amsterdam Java User Group. He has been part of Foojay from the very beginning as the "Father of Foojay": his first post, [foojay: A Place for Friends Of OpenJDK](/today/foojay-a-place-for-friends-of-openjdk/), dates from April 2020, and he has since contributed close to a hundred posts. Many of them keep the community running: the [Friends Of OpenJDK schedules at FOSDEM](/today/friends-of-openjdk-at-fosdem-2023/), the [virtual JUG tours](/today/virtual-foojay-jug-tour/), and the [status reports](/today/foojay-status-report-january-june-2022/) that show where the platform is heading.

Lately his writing has turned into hands-on exploration. There is a run of posts on observability, from [zero to OpenTelemetry](/today/from-zero-really-zero-to-opentelemetry/) to [observing Apache NetBeans with OpenTelemetry and Dash0](/today/observing-apache-netbeans-with-opentelemetry-and-dash0/). He also went through a [Temporal.io workshop on durable execution](/today/this-cant-possibly-work-what-i-learned-at-a-temporal-io-workshop-on-durable-execution/) and shared what he learned.

And then there is DuckDB. [Embedding DuckDB in a Maven app](/today/embedding-duckdb-in-a-maven-app-and-using-it-for-things-that-arent-databases/), [replacing in-memory loops in Spring Batch](/today/duckdb-in-spring-batch-replace-in-memory-java-loops-with-one-sql-statement/), [running its JDBC driver in a GraalVM native image](/today/running-duckdbs-jdbc-driver-in-a-graalvm-native-image/), and [NetBeans DataWrangler](/today/netbeans-datawrangler-query-convert-and-edit-data-analytics-files/) form a series worth reading in order, and more is on the way.

## Ricardo Mello

**[Ricardo Mello](/today/author/ricardo-mello/)** is a Senior Software Engineer focused on Java, Kotlin and MongoDB, helping developers build secure, scalable, and cloud-native applications. He joined Foojay in June 2025, and in that time has published 17 posts, a steady stream of practical, code-first guides.

His archive starts with the foundations: [MongoDB's aggregation framework for beginners](/today/mongodb-aggregation-framework-a-beginners-guide/), [clean and modular Java with a hexagonal architecture](/today/clean-and-modular-java-a-hexagonal-architecture-approach/), and a two-part look at [Hibernate ORM with MongoDB](/today/getting-started-with-hibernate-orm-and-mongodb/). Operational topics follow, such as the [MongoDB 8.0 migration guide](/today/mongodb-8-0-migration-guide-what-you-need-to-know-before-upgrading/) and [what to know before you shard a collection](/today/mongodb-sharding-what-to-know-before-you-shard/).

A second thread is search and AI: the three-part [Beyond Keywords](/today/beyond-keywords-implementing-semantic-search-in-java-with-spring-data-part-1/) series on semantic, vector and hybrid search in Java, [building a Kotlin app with Spring Boot and MongoDB Search](/today/building-a-kotlin-app-with-spring-boot-and-mongodb-search/), and the three-part [agentic warehouse management system](/today/building-an-agentic-warehouse-management-system-part-1-where-ai-agents-add-value/) built with Java and Spring AI.
