Are we still writing code, or are we mostly reviewing it? For many developers the answer changed within a year: the editor is no longer the center of the universe, the chat or the agent session is.

At the [JFall conference](https://jfall.nl/) in the Netherlands I asked four developers how AI changes their way of working: Adam Bien, Maaike van Putten, Sayf Jawad and Stéphane Philippart. In this episode I play those interviews and discuss them with Anton Arhipov, developer advocate at JetBrains and my guest in episode 91 about 25 years of IntelliJ IDEA.

We talk about why the quality and structure of your code base still matter when an agent writes the code, and how guardrails such as the compiler, static analysis and ArchUnit keep it under control. We look at how fast an AI-generated proof of concept can, and should, go to production, and where the next senior developers will come from. Then we discuss the cost of AI for society, your wallet and the planet, and whether local models are the answer. We end with how to use parallel AI sessions in a sensible way, how to let the AI explain what it did so you keep learning, and where IDEs and developer workflows could be heading.
