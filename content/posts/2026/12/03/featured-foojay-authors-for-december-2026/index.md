Foojay wouldn't exist without the many contributions from its community, whether it's a single post or an ongoing stream of knowledge sharing. That's why we highlight two of them each month as **[Featured Authors](/today/author/)**. After [Geertjan](/today/author/geertjan-wielenga/) and [Ricardo](/today/author/ricardo-mello/) in November, December belongs to **[Markus Spann](/today/author/markus-spann/)** and **[Angelo Rubini](/today/author/angelo-rubini/)**. Want to read more from either of them? Click their name to visit their Foojay author page, where you'll find their full list of posts.

## Markus Spann

**[Markus Spann](/today/author/markus-spann/)** has been loving Java from day one. He is the maintainer of UCanAccess and Jackcess, the author of iban-commons and JFairy, and a contributor to various other open source projects. His work on Foojay is recent, but it landed with a bang.

His first post, [UCanAccess: The Modern Pure-Java Bridge to Microsoft Access](/today/ucanaccess-java-ms-access-jdbc-guide/), explains how to connect Java applications to Access databases through a pure-Java, open source JDBC driver, on any platform.

His second, [Beyond mvn install: Maven Practices That Pay Off](/today/beyond-mvn-install-maven-practices/), turned out to be one of the most-read posts of the past few months. It collects techniques from POMs in daily use: fail fast, pin every version, let profiles switch themselves, and get an SBOM for free.

## Angelo Rubini

**[Angelo Rubini](/today/author/angelo-rubini/)** is a Senior Software Development Engineer and an enthusiastic member of the Jakarta EE community. Since January he has published five posts, and they follow two threads.

The first is Java on unikernels with Nanos. It starts with [Java and Jakarta EE and the Evolution of the Cloud with Nanos Unikernel](/today/java-jakarta-ee-and-the-evolution-of-the-cloud-with-nanos-unikernel/), continues with [profiling and troubleshooting a JVM running on Nanos](/today/unikernel-profiling-and-troubleshooting-jvm-on-nanos-unikernel/), and moves to [Java and Quarkus on AWS Graviton](/today/aws-nitro-and-cpu-graviton-meets-unikernels/). His most recent entry, [JVM on Unikernel Nanos: GC trade-offs and heap uncommit](/today/jvm-on-unikernel-nanos-gc-trade-offs-and-heap-uncommit/), tests five HotSpot garbage collectors with Eclipse Temurin 25 and Renaissance.

The second thread is Jakarta Faces. [Jakarta Faces, Java Web Framework, Stateless Edition](/today/jakarta-faces-java-web-framework-stateless-edition-marketplace-alifaces/) shows a marketplace built with server-side rendering and no HttpSession, no saved ViewState and no sticky sessions.
